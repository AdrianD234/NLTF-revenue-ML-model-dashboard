===========================================================================
PETROL VEHICLE TRAVEL MODEL - REVIEW PACK
===========================================================================

WHAT THIS MODEL DOES

It forecasts how far the average New Zealander drives in a petrol vehicle,
each quarter, measured in kilometres per person.

That number later feeds into a fuel excise revenue forecast, but this pack
covers only the driving-distance model. Nothing downstream of it.


WHAT IS IN THE BOX

  READ-ME-FIRST.txt          this file - the whole story
  ped-vkt-data.dta           the data, ready for Stata
  ped-vkt-data.csv           the same data as plain text
  run-in-stata.do            a starter script if you want one
  ped-vkt-forecast-test.csv  how well it has forecast in the past

Open the data, run whatever checks you like. The do-file is a convenience,
not a requirement.


===========================================================================
THE MODEL IN PLAIN ENGLISH
===========================================================================

Driving per person this quarter is explained by:

  - the real petrol price
  - real GDP per person
  - the unemployment rate
  - a long-run time trend, allowed to change slope from 2011
  - a step down from 2020 onward, plus an extra one-off allowance for 2020
  - the season (spring, summer, autumn, winter)
  - how much people drove LAST quarter

Everything except unemployment, the trend and the season dummies is in logs,
so those coefficients read as elasticities.

On top of that, the leftover errors are themselves modelled: whatever the
model got wrong last quarter, about half of it carries into this quarter.
That is the "AR(1)" part.


THE EQUATION

  log(driving per person)
      =  constant
       + b1 x log(real petrol price)
       + b2 x log(real GDP per person)
       + b3 x unemployment rate
       + b4 x trend
       + b5 x (trend, from 2011 onward)
       + b6 x (1 from 2020 onward)
       + b7 x (1 during 2020 only)
       + b8 x Q2  +  b9 x Q3  +  b10 x Q4
       + b11 x log(driving per person, last quarter)
       + error

  error this quarter = 0.523 x error last quarter + something new


THE ESTIMATED NUMBERS

  constant                                   1.9383
  log real petrol price                     -0.0628
  log real GDP per person                    0.1966
  unemployment rate                          0.1356
  trend                                     -0.0012
  trend from 2011                           -0.0001
  from 2020 onward                          -0.0237
  during 2020 only                          -0.0036
  Q2                                         0.0014
  Q3                                         0.0091
  Q4                                         0.0194
  driving last quarter                       0.5342

  error carry-over (the AR(1) term)          0.5233

Read the first three as elasticities. A 10 per cent rise in the real petrol
price is associated with about 0.63 per cent less driving in the same
quarter. A 10 per cent rise in real GDP per person is associated with about
2 per cent more driving.


===========================================================================
THE DATA
===========================================================================

97 quarters, 2002 Q1 to 2026 Q1.

The model itself uses 95 of them, 2002 Q2 to 2025 Q4. Two rows are excluded
and the reasons are simple:

  2002 Q1  needed only to supply "last quarter" for 2002 Q2
  2026 Q1  a placeholder with a zero, not a real observation

The column called "insample" is 1 for the 95 rows the model actually uses.
Filter on that and you have exactly the estimation sample.


THREE THINGS THAT WILL TRIP YOU UP IF NOBODY TELLS YOU

1. Unemployment is a DECIMAL, not a percentage.
   2002 Q1 shows 0.053, meaning 5.3 per cent. The column "unemppct" holds
   5.3 if you prefer that scale, but the model uses the decimal one.
   So the coefficient of 0.1356 means: unemployment up one percentage point
   is associated with driving up about 0.14 per cent.

2. Unemployment is NOT logged.
   Everything else that looks like a price or an income is logged.
   Unemployment is not. That was deliberate.

3. The trend counts quarters from the start of 2000.
   2000 Q1 is 1, 2000 Q2 is 2, and so on. It is not centred on anything and
   it does not restart.


WHAT THE COLUMNS MEAN

  period          the quarter, e.g. 2002Q2
  qdate           the same thing as a Stata date
  vktpc           kilometres driven per person - WHAT WE ARE EXPLAINING
  lnvktpc         the log of that - the actual dependent variable
  lnvktpclag1     last quarter's lnvktpc
  petrolprice     real petrol price, cents per litre
  lnpetrol        log of the above
  gdppc           real GDP per person, dollars
  lngdppc         log of the above
  unemppct        unemployment as a percentage, e.g. 5.3
  unemprate       unemployment as a decimal, e.g. 0.053 - THIS ONE IS USED
  population      included for context only - NOT in the equation
  trend           quarters since the start of 2000
  post2011        1 from 2011 onward
  post2011trend   trend multiplied by post2011
  post2020        1 from 2020 onward
  covid2020       1 during 2020 only
  q2, q3, q4      season markers - Q1 is the baseline
  insample        1 for the 95 rows the model uses


===========================================================================
HOW WELL HAS IT FORECAST?
===========================================================================

Tested by repeatedly standing at a past quarter, forecasting forward, and
comparing against what actually happened. Average error, ignoring sign:

  One quarter ahead        about 1 per cent
  One year ahead           about 2 to 3 per cent
  Three years ahead        about 4 to 5 per cent
  Five years ahead         about 6 per cent

Two things worth knowing:

  - Accuracy is solid out to about three years. Beyond that it has been
    tested but the evidence is thinner, and past five years it has not
    really been tested at all.

  - The errors are not centred on zero at long range. The model tends to
    forecast a bit HIGH the further out it goes, by around 4 per cent at
    the five-year mark.

The full quarter-by-quarter record is in ped-vkt-forecast-test.csv if you
want to check any of that yourself.


===========================================================================
THINGS I WOULD LOOK AT IF I WERE YOU
===========================================================================

These are honest question marks, not defects we are hiding. Some of them
may turn out to be fine.

1. Unemployment has a POSITIVE sign.
   The model says higher unemployment goes with MORE driving. That is the
   opposite of what most people would expect. It may be picking up
   something else. Worth poking at.

2. Both "last quarter's driving" and a carried-over error are in there.
   Their sizes are almost the same, 0.53 and 0.52. Two things doing a
   similar job raises the question of whether both are needed.

3. The trend does a lot of work.
   There is a trend, plus a slope change at 2011, plus a step at 2020.
   Those dates were chosen, not discovered. Whether they are the right
   dates is a fair question.

4. The petrol price effect looks small.
   Minus 0.063 in the same quarter is low compared with most published
   estimates. Because last quarter's driving is in the equation, the full
   long-run effect is larger, roughly minus 0.13, but that is still low.

5. Turning logs back into kilometres.
   We simply take the exponential. That gives a middle-of-the-road
   forecast rather than an average one. For revenue work the average is
   usually what you want.


===========================================================================
ONE THING TO BE CLEAR ABOUT
===========================================================================

The model and the data are stable and reproducible. Everyone looking at
this is looking at exactly the same thing.

That is NOT the same as saying the model is right. Whether the economics
stack up is exactly what we are asking you to judge.
